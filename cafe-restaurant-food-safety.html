What NSW Food Authority requires from cafes and restaurants

Cafes and restaurants are classified as Class 2 food businesses under the NSW Food Act 2003. This means you are legally required to maintain a documented Food Safety Program based on HACCP principles under Standard 3.2.1 of the Food Standards Code.

Additionally, Standard 3.2.2A (fully implemented in 2023) requires all food service businesses handling unpackaged potentially hazardous food to have a certified Food Safety Supervisor who is reasonably available to supervise operations.

Allergen management after the 2021 FSANZ changes

The 2021 amendments to the Food Standards Code elevated sesame to mandatory major allergen status and strengthened the requirements around advisory statements. If your Food Safety Program was written before 2022 and hasn't been reviewed, your allergen management section is almost certainly non-compliant.

For a typical Sydney cafe menu — sourdough bread, tahini-based dressings, nut-containing cakes, and multiple egg preparations — allergen cross-contact risk requires systematic assessment and documented controls. We build this into every Food Safety Program we develop.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a Food Safety Program if I only sell coffee and pre-packaged food?
If you only sell pre-packaged food and beverages without any food handling or preparation, you may be classified as Class 3 or 4 and not require a documented FSP. However, if you prepare any beverages using dairy (milk-based coffees), you should confirm your classification with the NSW Food Authority. Most cafes that prepare any food on-site are Class 2 and require a documented FSP.
How often does my cafe's Food Safety Program need to be reviewed?
Under Standard 3.2.1, your Food Safety Program must be reviewed at least annually and whenever a significant change occurs — new menu items, new equipment, new premises, or changes to food safety regulations. Following the 2021 FSANZ allergen amendments, any FSP written before 2022 should have been reviewed and updated already.
What happens if my probe thermometer is found to be uncalibrated during an audit?
An uncalibrated probe thermometer during a NSW Food Authority audit will typically result in a non-compliance finding. All monitoring records taken with that thermometer may be considered unreliable, which could indicate the CCP was not being adequately controlled. Weekly ice slurry calibration checks (0°C ± 0.5°C) take less than one minute and should be standard procedure.
Does my Food Safety Supervisor need to be present every shift?
No. Under Standard 3.2.2A, the FSS must be reasonably available to supervise food handling operations — they do not need to be physically present at all times. However, they must be contactable and able to provide oversight when needed. The FSS must hold a current certificate from an approved RTO.
