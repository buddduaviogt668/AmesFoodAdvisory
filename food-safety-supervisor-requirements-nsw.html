Food Safety Supervisor Requirements in NSW

Who must appoint a Food Safety Supervisor, what the role requires, and the most common compliance mistakes NSW food businesses make under Standard 3.2.2A.

Updated June 2026 · 7 min read · AMES Food Advisory

The Food Safety Supervisor (FSS) requirement is one of the most frequently misunderstood compliance obligations for NSW food businesses. Since Standard 3.2.2A came into force, many businesses either think they don't need one when they do, have appointed someone who doesn't meet the requirements, or have a certificate on file but no one actually performing the role. Council inspectors are increasingly focused on all three.

What is a Food Safety Supervisor?

A Food Safety Supervisor is a person within your food business who holds a current, nationally recognised food safety qualification and is responsible for ensuring your food handlers have adequate food safety skills and knowledge. The role exists to ensure there is always an accountable, qualified person who can guide, correct, and advise food handlers on safe food handling practices.

The FSS is not a paper credential — they must be genuinely reachable during food handling operations and able to actively guide staff on food safety matters.

Which NSW food businesses need a Food Safety Supervisor?

Under Standard 3.2.2A of the FSANZ Food Standards Code, the FSS requirement applies to food businesses in two categories:

FSS Required

Food service or catering businesses

Restaurants, cafes, takeaways, caterers, food trucks, school canteens, hospital food services, aged care food services, function centres, pubs and clubs that serve food.

FSS Required

Food processing businesses

Businesses that process food intended for retail sale, including bakeries, smallgoods producers, meal kit manufacturers, juice manufacturers, and other food processors.

Check Required

Food retail only (low risk)

Businesses that only sell pre-packaged, shelf-stable food without any handling may not require an FSS. Check Standard 3.2.2A for your specific activities — when in doubt, appoint one.

Exemptions Apply

Certain charitable and community events

Some temporary and community food events have modified requirements. Check with your local council EHO for the specific requirements that apply to your event type.

When in doubt — appoint one

If your business handles any potentially hazardous food (food that requires temperature control to remain safe) and serves it to the public, you almost certainly need a Food Safety Supervisor. Appointing one when you don't strictly need to has no downside. Not appointing one when you do is a compliance breach that will be noted on inspection.

What are the FSS qualification requirements?

The Food Safety Supervisor must hold a Statement of Attainment from a Registered Training Organisation (RTO) that confirms competency in the following nationally recognised units:

  • SITXFSA005 — Use hygienic practices for food safety
  • SITXFSA006 — Participate in safe food handling practices

These units are delivered by RTOs across NSW as part of the Food Safety Supervisor course, typically completed in one day either in person or online. The Statement of Attainment issued by the RTO is the document you must be able to produce on request.

Note that not all food safety training certificates satisfy Standard 3.2.2A. The training must be provided by a registered RTO and the Statement of Attainment must reference the specific SITXFSA unit codes above. Generic food safety awareness training does not meet the requirement.

The 4 legal requirements for the FSS role

  • ✓ Holds a current FSS qualification. The Statement of Attainment from a registered RTO confirming competency in SITXFSA005 and SITXFSA006 must be current and on file at the premises.
  • ✓ Is an employee or owner/operator of the business. The FSS cannot be a consultant or external party. They must be employed by or own the business.
  • ✓ Is contactable during food handling hours. The FSS must be reachable by food handlers at all times the business is handling food. A qualified FSS who is on indefinite leave or in a different city is not compliant.
  • ✓ Actively guides food handlers. The role is not passive. The FSS must ensure food handlers understand and follow safe food handling practices — and must be able to demonstrate this at inspection.

The most common FSS compliance mistakes in NSW

The FSS has left the business

Staff turnover is the number one cause of FSS non-compliance. When the person who holds the certificate resigns, the business loses its compliant FSS overnight. If you have only one qualified person, you are one resignation away from a compliance breach. Businesses should have at least two qualified FSS staff, or have a plan for fast replacement.

The certificate is from an unregistered provider

Online food safety courses are widespread, and not all of them are delivered by registered RTOs. A certificate from a non-RTO provider — however professional-looking — does not satisfy Standard 3.2.2A. Before enrolling, verify the provider is registered on training.gov.au.

The FSS is in the role but not the role

Having a framed certificate on the wall is not FSS compliance. The FSS must actively guide staff, be reachable during service, and be able to demonstrate food safety oversight at inspection. An inspector may ask the nominated FSS questions about your Food Safety Program, your hazard controls, and your corrective action procedures. If they can't answer, the appointment is not genuine compliance.

The certificate references outdated unit codes

Training unit codes are updated periodically by the national vocational training framework. Older certificates may reference superseded unit codes (e.g. SITXFSA001/002). Check with your council or the NSW Food Authority whether older certificates remain accepted under their current inspection framework.

How to appoint or replace a Food Safety Supervisor

  1. Identify a suitable employee (or owner/operator) — someone who is present during food operations and likely to stay with the business.
  2. Enrol them in a Food Safety Supervisor course delivered by a registered RTO. Confirm the course includes SITXFSA005 and SITXFSA006.
  3. Obtain the Statement of Attainment on completion. File it at the premises and make a copy for off-site records.
  4. Brief the FSS on their responsibilities — they need to understand your Food Safety Program, your CCPs, your temperature controls, and your corrective action procedures.
  5. If you need your FSS to also understand your full FSP and HACCP documentation, AMES Food Advisory's food safety training service can deliver this as an on-site session.

Frequently asked questions

Does every food business in NSW need a Food Safety Supervisor?

No. Under Standard 3.2.2A, the Food Safety Supervisor requirement applies to food service businesses and caterers that handle food served ready-to-eat or potentially hazardous. Businesses that only handle shelf-stable, pre-packaged food are not required to have an FSS. When in doubt, check with your local council EHO or the NSW Food Authority.

Can the Food Safety Supervisor be an external consultant?

No. The Food Safety Supervisor must be an employee or the owner/operator of the business — they cannot be an external consultant. The FSS must also be contactable by food handlers at the business at all times the business is handling food.

How long is a Food Safety Supervisor certificate valid in NSW?

FSS certificates do not have a fixed expiry date under Standard 3.2.2A. However, councils and the NSW Food Authority assess whether the certificate is 'current' at the time of inspection. If your FSS certificate is more than 5 years old and your business has changed significantly, an inspector may question its adequacy. Many industry bodies recommend renewal every 5 years as a practical standard.

What happens if my Food Safety Supervisor leaves the business?

If your Food Safety Supervisor leaves, you must appoint and train a replacement as soon as practicable. There is no formal grace period in Standard 3.2.2A, but inspectors consider the steps the business has taken to address the gap. Having a second staff member in training at all times is the safest approach.
