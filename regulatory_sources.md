# Verified regulatory sources used for growth pages

## NSW Food Authority
URL: https://www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/industry/audits-and-compliance/food-safety-programs-haccp

The official NSW Food Authority page identifies HACCP as Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Point and describes food safety programs as systems that establish, document and verify controls, with prerequisite programs and regular review.

## Food Standards Australia New Zealand — Standard 3.2.1
URL: https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/business/food-safety-standards/standard-321-food-safety-programs

FSANZ states that a food safety program is a written document indicating how a food business controls hazards associated with its food handling activities. The page explains that only certain high-risk businesses are required under the national standard, including businesses serving or processing potentially hazardous food for vulnerable people, and notes that state or territory legislation may also create requirements.

## FSANZ — Food Safety Standards Chapter 3
URL: https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/business/food-safety-standards

FSANZ describes Standard 3.2.1 as setting general requirements for food safety programs, while Standard 3.2.2 covers food handling controls, skills and knowledge, hygiene, cleaning and maintenance. FSANZ also notes that some exemptions can apply to private-home and temporary-event businesses, so the local regulator should be contacted for operation-specific advice.

## Copy guardrails

The HACCP Simplified page uses conservative wording: requirements depend on the business type and applicable state or territory rules; businesses should confirm their position with the relevant regulator or council. It does not claim that every NSW food business must hold the same documented program.
