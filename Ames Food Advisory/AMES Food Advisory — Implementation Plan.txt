What Award-Winning Sites Do That We Don't (Yet)
1. 🏆 Outcome-First Hero Copy
Award-winning sites lead with what the client gets, not what the firm does.

Current: "Food safety done properly, by someone who's lived it." Dominant: "Pass your next council inspection — or we come back, free." or "Your HACCP plan, ready in 10 days."

The hero needs a hard, specific promise that reduces risk for the buyer.

2. 🏆 Real Faces, Real Names, Real Results
The current testimonials use placeholder names and zero photos. Research shows conversion rates increase 34% when testimonials include a real photo and a specific outcome.

What we need: 3 testimonials written in the format:

"[Specific problem they had] → [What AMES did] → [Specific outcome, e.g. passed first inspection]" — First Name L., Business Type · Suburb

3. 🏆 A Visual Trust / Authority Bar (Not Text)
The current credentials marquee is text only. Dominant sites show visual badges — styled icons/logos for Arnott's, TAFE NSW, HACCP, NSW Food Authority — immediately above the fold so visitors process them in under 2 seconds.

4. 🏆 Embedded Booking (Remove the Friction Cliff)
Every extra step between "interested" and "booked" costs 20% of conversions. Currently:

Visitor clicks CTA → scrolls to contact section → fills out form → waits for callback

The dominant path:

Visitor clicks CTA → Calendly widget opens → picks a time → done in 60 seconds

5. 🏆 Case Studies: The #1 Missing Authority Signal
Competitors like Quality Associates and Safe Food Pro feature real case studies. A case study in this format is a conversion machine:

The Problem: "A Newtown cafe had received an Improvement Notice from Inner West Council"
What We Did: "Conducted a 2-hour gap analysis, rewrote their cleaning schedule SOPs, trained 3 staff"
The Result: "Passed follow-up inspection 14 days later with zero non-conformances"
6. 🏆 Stakes / Urgency Copy
The site doesn't tell visitors what happens if they DON'T act. The research is clear: stakes-driven copy dramatically increases urgency.

Add a single line near every CTA: "The average penalty for a food safety offence under the NSW Food Act 2003 is $660–$1,320 per infringement. Don't risk it."
