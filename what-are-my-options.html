Why “just ask the council” doesn't work

It isn't that council staff don't want to help — they do. The problem is structural. Council regulates your premises (planning, plumbing, grease traps, AS 4674 fit-out), while the NSW Food Authority regulates your food handling (notification, classification, food safety programs, Food Safety Supervisors). Front-line officers see their own half of the picture, so the advice you receive is locally correct and globally incomplete.

That's why two new food businesses on the same street can walk away with completely different instructions. Neither is being misinformed — each is hearing one layer of a three-layer answer.

The expensive version of this problem

The most costly version appears when you act on partial advice. Registering before you understand your classification, trading before your food safety program exists, or submitting a template program that describes a kitchen you don't have — then discovering the gap at your first inspection, when it's far too late to redo it quietly.

The fix is sequencing. Get the full picture once, in the right order, from someone who works in NSW food regulation every day — then execute with confidence. Common questions

Straight answers

Why does the council give different answers about starting a food business?
Because the answer is split across three layers of government. Your local council regulates your premises — planning, plumbing and fit-out — while the NSW Food Authority handles food business notification and classification under the NSW Food Act 2003, and the Food Standards Code sets food safety program requirements. Front-line staff usually only see their own layer, so new business owners get partial answers that are each technically correct but incomplete when stitched together.
How do I know what class my food business is?
Your classification (Class 1, 2 or 3) depends on what food you handle, how it is processed, and who you sell it to. Class 1 serves vulnerable populations, Class 2 handles ready-to-eat food that requires a documented food safety program, and Class 3 is lower-risk retail. Misclassification is one of the most common startup mistakes because it changes your notification, your food safety program and your inspection obligations. The options assessment above gives you your likely class in 60 seconds; the scoping session confirms it definitively.
