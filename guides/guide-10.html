Food Recall Plan Template and Guide Australia

A documented food recall plan is a legal requirement for all Australian food businesses under Standard 3.2.2 — Food Safety Practices and General Requirements (Division 6) and Standard 3.2.2A — Food Safety Management Tools of the Australia New Zealand Food Standards Code. If your business supplies, manufactures, or distributes food to other businesses or directly to consumers, you must have a written recall plan that can be activated at any time.

The NSW Food Authority works closely with FSANZ (Food Standards Australia New Zealand) in coordinating food recalls. Under the Food Regulation 2015 (NSW), failure to have a compliant recall plan or failure to initiate a recall when required can result in significant penalties, including court-imposed fines and enforcement orders.

This guide provides a complete overview of the recall plan components required under Australian law, the role of FSANZ in recall coordination, traceability requirements, mock recall procedures, and steps for plan activation.

Legal Requirement for a Recall Plan

Under Standard 3.2.2, clause 12, food businesses that manufacture, process, or distribute food must have a system in place to ensure that unsafe food can be traced and recalled from the supply chain. Standard 3.2.2A goes further, requiring businesses handling potentially hazardous food to have documented food safety management tools, which includes a recall plan.

The plan must be written, current, and capable of being implemented immediately. The NSW Food Authority can request to see your recall plan during a routine inspection or as part of a compliance audit. If you do not have a plan, or if your plan is out of date or untested, you may receive a non-compliance notice.

Key Components of a Food Recall Plan

Your recall plan must include the following sections as a minimum:

1. Recall Team and Contact Details

Identify the recall coordinator and alternates. Include out-of-hours contact numbers. The recall coordinator should have the authority to make decisions on product withdrawal and public communication.

2. Product Identification and Description

List all products your business produces or distributes, including batch numbers, date codes, and barcodes (GTIN). Include product descriptions, pack sizes, and shelf life information.

3. Traceability System

Describe how you trace product one step forward (customer) and one step back (supplier). This is the foundation of a recall and must be tested through mock recalls. Your traceability system should allow you to identify within 4 hours where a specific batch of product was sent and from whom it was received.

4. Recall Classification

Define how you classify a recall event. FSANZ recognises three recall types:

  • Trade recall: Product is recalled from wholesalers, distributors, and retailers. Not directly from consumers.
  • Consumer recall: Product is recalled from all points in the supply chain, including consumers through public notification.
  • Withdrawal: Product is removed from the supply chain for reasons other than food safety (e.g., quality issues, labelling errors that do not present a food safety risk). Withdrawals are not coordinated by FSANZ.

5. Communication Plan

Include templates for:

  • Internal notification to staff and management
  • External communication to customers, suppliers, and distributors
  • Public notification through FSANZ (if required)
  • Media statement template
  • NSW Food Authority notification procedure

6. Product Disposal and Reconciliation

Describe how recalled product will be stored, segregated, and disposed of. Detail the reconciliation process to track the percentage of recalled product recovered at each level of the supply chain.

7. Post-Recall Review

Include a procedure for conducting a post-recall review to identify root cause and implement corrective actions to prevent recurrence.

The Role of FSANZ in Food Recalls

FSANZ coordinates food recalls across Australia and New Zealand. When a food recall is initiated, FSANZ:

  • Publishes recall notices on the FSANZ Food Recall website
  • Coordinates with state and territory food enforcement agencies (including the NSW Food Authority)
  • Issues media alerts for consumer-level recalls
  • Maintains the national food recall database

If you determine a food safety issue with your product, you must notify the NSW Food Authority immediately. Do not wait for a complaint to escalate. Early notification is a legal obligation and demonstrates due diligence.

Mock Recall Requirements

A recall plan that has never been tested is not reliable. The NSW Food Authority and third-party auditors (such as SQF, BRC, or GFSI certification bodies) require that food businesses conduct at least one mock recall per year. During a mock recall, you must:

  • Select a specific batch or date code of a product
  • Trace the product one step forward and one step back within the required timeframe (typically 4 hours)
  • Document the results, including any gaps or delays identified
  • Review and update the recall plan based on lessons learned

Mock recalls must be recorded and retained as part of your food safety documentation. Our internal auditing services include conducting mock recall exercises and providing a detailed report on your traceability performance.

Traceability: One Step Forward, One Step Back

Traceability is the backbone of any recall. Your system must enable you to identify:

  • One step back (supplier): From whom did you source the raw ingredients or finished product? Include batch numbers, supplier name, date of receipt.
  • One step forward (customer): To whom did you supply the finished product? Include customer name, delivery date, quantity, batch/lot numbers.

This applies at every stage of the supply chain. If you are a café that also wholesales house-made products to other businesses, you must be able to trace both your incoming ingredients and your outgoing wholesale goods.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a food recall plan mandatory for a small café or restaurant?

If you are a retailer selling directly to consumers and do not supply other businesses, the requirement for a written recall plan may be less prescriptive. However, if your café manufactures and supplies products to other businesses (e.g., wholesale cakes, sauces), you must have a documented recall plan. Additionally, Standard 3.2.2A requires businesses handling potentially hazardous food to have documented food safety management tools, which includes traceability and recall procedures. We recommend all food businesses have a basic recall plan as a matter of due diligence.

How long should I keep recall plan records?

Mock recall records, actual recall documentation, and traceability testing results should be retained for a minimum of 2 years. FSANZ recommends keeping batch production and distribution records for the shelf life of the product plus 6 months. Many third-party certification schemes require 3–5 years of traceability records.

What is the difference between a food recall and a food withdrawal?

A food recall is the removal of food from the supply chain due to a food safety concern (e.g., contamination, undeclared allergen, microbiological hazard). Recalls are coordinated by FSANZ and are publicly notified. A withdrawal is the removal of food for reasons other than food safety (e.g., quality defects, labelling errors that do not present a safety risk). Withdrawals are not publicly listed and are managed directly between the business and its customers.
