Staff Training Requirements for Food Handlers in NSW

Food safety training is not optional in NSW. Under Standard 3.2.2 (Food Safety Practices and General Requirements) of the Australia New Zealand Food Standards Code, every food business must ensure that food handlers have the skills and knowledge needed to handle food safely. In practice, this means you must train your staff—and you must be able to prove it to your local council or the NSW Food Authority during an inspection.

This guide covers who needs training, what the Food Safety Supervisor (FSS) role requires, how to document training, and when refresher training is needed. For hands-on support, explore our food safety training services.

What the Law Says: Skills and Knowledge Requirements

Clause 3 of Standard 3.2.2 states: “A food business must ensure that persons undertaking or supervising food handling operations have appropriate skills and knowledge in food safety and food hygiene for the work they do.” This is deliberately broad: the required level of training must match the risk level of the work.

A staff member who only makes coffee requires different knowledge than a chef handling raw poultry and managing allergen cross-contact. However, every food handler must understand:

  • The basic microbiology of food poisoning and high-risk foods
  • Personal hygiene requirements (handwashing, clean clothing, illness reporting)
  • Temperature control: keeping cold food at or below 5°C and hot food at or above 60°C
  • Cross-contamination prevention (separate chopping boards, storage separation)
  • Allergen awareness and communication
  • Cleaning and sanitising procedures
  • How to report a food safety concern

The NSW Food Authority expects that this training is documented, verified, and refreshed regularly. A verbal instruction on day one is not sufficient. Formal training records are required to demonstrate compliance during an audit or inspection.

Food Safety Supervisor (FSS) Requirements in NSW

In addition to general food handler training, NSW imposes a specific Food Safety Supervisor (FSS) requirement under the Food Regulation 2015. If your business is licensed by the NSW Food Authority, or if it is a retail or food service business in the jurisdiction of a local council, you must nominate at least one FSS who has completed an approved training course.

The FSS must:

  • Hold a Statement of Attainment for the nationally accredited unit SITXFSA005 (Use hygienic practices for food safety) or equivalent, plus a supervisory-level unit such as SITXFSA006 (Participate in safe food handling practices) or SITXFSA008 (Develop and implement a food safety program).
  • Be reasonably available at all times the business operates. For businesses with multiple shifts, you may need more than one FSS.
  • Oversee the day-to-day food safety operations, including temperature monitoring, cleaning records, and staff training.
  • Be retrained every 5 years (the FSS certification expires after 5 years in NSW).

The FSS requirement applies to all retail and food service businesses, including cafes, restaurants, takeaway shops, bakeries, caterers, and manufacturers. If you are unsure whether you need an FSS, see our guide on notification vs licensing.

Developing a Training Register

A training register (or training matrix) is your proof that staff have received appropriate training. During a council inspection or NSW Food Authority audit, you will be asked to produce it. A robust training register should include:

  • Employee name and role
  • Date of training
  • Training topic or unit code (e.g. SITXFSA005, allergen awareness, temperature monitoring)
  • Trainer name and credentials
  • Assessment method (observation, written test, verbal questioning)
  • Outcome (competent / not yet competent)
  • Refresher due date
  • Signature of employee and trainer

Your training register should be reviewed quarterly to identify upcoming refresher requirements. For new employees, induction training should be completed before they begin handling food. Our SOP writing service can help you document your training procedures.

Refresher Training Intervals

Training is not a one-time event. Knowledge fades, regulations change, and new risks emerge. Best practice in NSW is to provide refresher training at the following intervals:

  • Annual refresher: All food handlers should receive an annual update covering key topics: handwashing, temperature control, allergen management, and any changes to the Food Safety Program or menu. This can be delivered as a 30-minute toolbox talk or online module.
  • FSS re-certification: Every 5 years (mandatory under NSW regulations). The FSS must re-attend the approved training course to maintain their supervisory qualification.
  • After an incident: If a food poisoning complaint, critical non-conformance, or enforcement action occurs, immediate refresher training should be conducted for all relevant staff.
  • After a menu or equipment change: Introduce new training whenever high-risk menu items or new equipment (e.g. a blast chiller, vacuum packer) are added.

Refresher training should always be documented in the training register. Even an informal 10-minute discussion should be recorded with date, topic, and attendee names. If it isn’t documented, it didn’t happen from a regulatory perspective.

Induction Training for New Hires

Every new food handler must complete an induction before handling food unsupervised. The induction should cover your business-specific Food Safety Program, the location of temperature monitoring equipment and cleaning supplies, the handwashing protocol, the illness reporting procedure, and any menu-specific allergen risks. The induction should be signed off by both the employee and the FSS, and the record filed in the training register. Without a documented induction, you cannot prove that a new staff member had the required skills and knowledge before starting work.

We recommend a structured induction checklist that aligns with your SOPs. This ensures consistency across all new hires and simplifies the documentation process for audits. Our startup package includes induction templates customised for cafe, restaurant, and manufacturing environments.

Record Retention for Training

Under the Food Regulation 2015 (NSW), training records must be retained for at least 12 months after the training is delivered. However, given that FSS certification lasts for 5 years, we recommend retaining all training records for at least the duration of the employee’s employment plus 12 months. This ensures you can demonstrate historical compliance if there is a retrospective investigation. For licensed businesses, the NSW Food Authority may require longer retention periods as a condition of your licence.

Consequences of Non-Compliance

Failing to train staff adequately can have serious consequences. Councils in NSW routinely issue penalty infringement notices (PINs) for failures in training documentation. The NSW Food Authority can impose penalties of up to $1,320 for individuals and $6,600 for corporations under the Food Act 2003 (NSW) for breaches of Standard 3.2.2. More importantly, inadequately trained staff are the leading cause of foodborne illness outbreaks in the hospitality sector.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

Does every staff member need a formal food safety certificate?
No, only the Food Safety Supervisor (FSS) is required to hold a formal Statement of Attainment from an accredited training course. General food handlers must have appropriate skills and knowledge for their role, which can be delivered through in-house training, as long as it is documented. However, we recommend that at least key staff (chefs, managers, supervisors) hold formal certification to strengthen your compliance position.
How long does FSS certification last in NSW?
FSS certification is valid for 5 years in NSW. After that period, the FSS must re-attend and re-complete the approved training course to renew their certification. It is the business owner’s responsibility to track FSS expiry dates and schedule renewal training before the certificate lapses.
Can training be delivered online, or does it need to be in person?
Food handler training can be delivered online, in person, or through a blended approach. For general food handler skills and knowledge, online modules are acceptable. However, the FSS qualification required in NSW typically involves a face-to-face or virtual classroom component with a qualified assessor, as the competencies require demonstration of practical skills. Check with your training provider that their course is accepted by the NSW Food Authority.
