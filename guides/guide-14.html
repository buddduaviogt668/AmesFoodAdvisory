Food Business Notification vs Licensing in NSW

One of the most common questions we hear from new food business owners in NSW is: “Do I need to notify my local council, or do I need a licence from the NSW Food Authority?” The answer depends entirely on the nature and risk level of your food operations. Getting it wrong can result in fines, enforcement action, or even closure of your business.

This guide explains the two-tier regulatory system in NSW, helps you determine which category your business falls into, and walks through the application processes for both notification and licensing. If you are starting a new venture, our food business startup package can help you navigate these requirements from day one.

The Two-Tier System: Notification vs Licensing

NSW operates a bifurcated food regulation system under the Food Act 2003 (NSW) and the Food Regulation 2015:

  • Notification (with your local council) applies to lower-risk retail and food service businesses. This includes most cafes, restaurants, takeaway shops, bakeries, and delicatessens.
  • Licensing (with the NSW Food Authority) applies to higher-risk operations, including food manufacturing, catering, food service to vulnerable populations, and businesses operating across multiple council areas.

The NSW Food Authority is the state-level regulator responsible for licensing, compliance monitoring, and enforcement across the entire food supply chain. Local councils are responsible for inspecting and enforcing food safety standards for notified (unlicensed) food businesses within their area. The food safety consultants at AMES regularly assist businesses with both pathways.

When Does a Business Need to Notify Their Local Council?

Under the Food Regulation 2015, most retail and food service businesses must notify their local council before commencing operations. Notification is required for:

  • Cafes, restaurants, and takeaway shops
  • Pubs, clubs, and bars serving food
  • Bakeries, butcheries, and fishmongers (retail only)
  • Delicatessens and grocery stores selling unpackaged food
  • Mobile food vendors and food trucks (notified with the council where the business is based)
  • Home-based food businesses selling direct to the public
  • Temporary and seasonal food stalls (e.g. at markets, fairs, festivals)

Notification is a relatively simple process. You provide your business details to the council, pay a one-off notification fee (typically $100–$300 depending on the council), and you will be added to the council’s register of food businesses. Once notified, your business will be subject to routine inspections by council Environmental Health Officers (EHOs). For more on what to expect, see our council readiness checklist.

Important: Notification does not grant permission to operate. You must also comply with all other relevant legislation, including planning approvals, building codes, water approvals, and the Food Standards Code. A council EHO will inspect your premises before or shortly after you open to ensure compliance.

Which Businesses Require NSW Food Authority Licensing?

Licensing is required for higher-risk food businesses as defined under the Food Regulation 2015. The following activities must be licensed by the NSW Food Authority:

  • Food manufacturing (including preparation, processing, packing, and storage of food for sale to other businesses or the public) – with narrow exemptions for retail-only activities.
  • Catering operations that prepare food for events, functions, or off-site service (including commercial caterers, function centres, and institutional caterers).
  • Food service to vulnerable populations, including hospitals, aged care facilities, childcare centres, and maternity hospitals.
  • Retailers that also manufacture – for example, a cafe that roasts its own coffee and packages it for retail sale may require a manufacturing licence if the packaged product is supplied wholesale.
  • Primary production processing (certain activities such as egg grading, dairy processing, meat processing).
  • Any business operating in multiple council areas (to avoid multiple council notifications, the NSW Food Authority acts as the single regulator).

Licensed businesses must develop and implement a Food Safety Program based on HACCP principles, as required under Standard 3.2.1 of the Food Standards Code. They are also subject to regular audits by the NSW Food Authority or approved third-party auditors. The Notification Process Step by Step

  1. Identify your local council: Use the NSW Office of Local Government’s council finder to identify the council for your business address.
  2. Complete the notification form: Most councils provide a Food Business Notification form on their website. You will need your business name, ABN, address, contact details, and a description of the food activities.
  3. Pay the fee: Notification fees vary by council. Pay online or at the council office.
  4. Receive your registration number: The council will issue a notification number (sometimes called a food business registration number). Display this on your premises if required.
  5. Prepare for inspection: The council EHO will typically schedule an initial inspection within the first 6–12 weeks of operation.

For detailed guidance on preparing for your first council inspection, see our guide for mobile food vendors or our council readiness checklist.

The Licensing Process for Higher-Risk Operations

  1. Determine if you need a licence: Review the NSW Food Authority’s “Do I Need a Licence?” tool on their website, or speak to our consultants for a definitive assessment.
  2. Develop a Food Safety Program: You must prepare a Food Safety Program (FSP) based on HACCP principles, covering all your food handling activities. The FSP must be approved by the NSW Food Authority.
  3. Submit your application: Apply through the NSW Food Authority’s online portal. You must include your FSP, business details, site plans, and the applicable fee.
  4. Pre-licensing inspection: The NSW Food Authority will conduct an inspection of your premises before issuing the licence. This verifies that your FSP can be implemented as written.
  5. Receive your licence: Once approved, you will receive a food business licence with specific conditions. Your licence must be renewed annually.

The cost of a food business licence varies depending on the risk classification (typically $500–$2,500 per year). The licence is specific to the premises and the activity described in the application. If you change premises or significantly change your operations, you must notify the NSW Food Authority.

Consequences of Operating Without the Correct Registration

Operating a food business in NSW without proper notification or licensing is an offence under the Food Act 2003 (NSW). Penalties include:

  • Penalty Infringement Notices (PINs) of up to $1,320 for individuals and $6,600 for corporations
  • Prohibition orders preventing you from handling food
  • Criminal prosecution for serious or persistent breaches
  • Closure of the business

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

I run a home-based cake business in Sydney. Do I need to notify my council?
Yes. A home-based food business that bakes cakes, slices, cookies, or other food for sale to the public must notify the local council where the home is located. You do not need a NSW Food Authority licence unless you are supplying wholesale to other businesses or manufacturing above certain thresholds. Notification is typically straightforward, but the council will inspect your home kitchen to ensure it meets food safety standards.
Can I operate my food truck under my restaurant’s existing notification?
No. A mobile food business (food truck, trailer, or van) is considered a separate food premises from your restaurant. Even if you operate both from the same business entity, the food truck must be separately notified with the council where the vehicle is garaged. Some councils also require a separate approval for each market or event location where you trade. See our guide for mobile food vendors for detailed requirements.
What is the difference between a Food Safety Program and a licence?
The licence is the regulatory approval granted by the NSW Food Authority that permits you to operate a specific food business activity. The Food Safety Program (FSP) is the document you must develop and submit as part of your licence application. The FSP describes how you will manage food safety hazards (based on HACCP principles) and is a condition of your licence. Without an approved FSP, the NSW Food Authority will not issue a licence.
