What Auditors and Inspectors Actually Check

Auditors assess your food safety system across three core domains: documentation, physical premises, and people. Understanding what they look for in each area is the first step to audit readiness.

1. Documentation Review

Your paperwork tells the story of your food safety culture. Auditors will examine:

  • Food Safety Program (FSP): Your HACCP-based plan must be site-specific, validated, and current. If you need a compliant plan, see our HACCP plan development service.
  • Temperature logs: Receiving, storage, cooking, cooling, and transport temperature records for the past 3â€“6 months.
  • Cleaning schedules and records: Daily, weekly, and periodic cleaning tasks must be documented with sign-off.
  • Training records: Evidence that all food handlers have completed food safety training appropriate to their role. Our food safety training Sydney courses are designed to meet this requirement.
  • Supplier approval records: You must demonstrate that your ingredient suppliers are approved and monitored.
  • Corrective action reports: Any time a critical limit is breached, the corrective action must be documented. Read our dedicated guide to corrective action procedures for detailed guidance.

2. Physical Inspection Points

Auditors will walk your premises with a keen eye. They are checking for:

  • Pest control: No signs of rodent or insect activity. Bait stations must be numbered, mapped, and serviced by a licensed pest controller.
  • Hand washing facilities: Dedicated hand wash basins with warm running water, soap, and single-use towels must be accessible. No food in sinks.
  • Temperature control equipment: Fridges at 5Â°C or below, freezers at -15Â°C or below (or -18Â°C for ice cream). Calibrated thermometers in each unit.
  • Food storage: Raw and ready-to-eat foods separated. All food off the floor, covered, and labelled with date codes.
  • Sanitary design: Walls, floors, and ceilings in good repair. No cracks, flaking paint, or harbourage points.
  • Waste management: Bins covered, regularly emptied, and located away from food handling areas.

3. Staff Interviews

Auditors will speak directly with your team â€” not just the manager. They want to see that food safety knowledge flows through every level of the business. Typical questions include:

  • "What is the correct temperature for receiving chilled chicken?"
  • "How do you wash and sanitise chopping boards?"
  • "What do you do if you find a customer's meal contains an allergen?"
  • "Show me how you calibrate the probe thermometer."
  • "How often do you change the sanitiser solution in the dishwashing sink?"

Your staff's answers must align with your written procedures. Discrepancies between what the paperwork says and what staff do is one of the most common root causes of major non-conformances. Our SOP writing service ensures that your procedures are clear, practical, and trainable.

The Complete NSW Food Safety Audit Checklist

Use the checklist below to run a pre-audit self-inspection. Tick off each item before your official audit date.

Pre-Audit Preparation

  • Confirm audit date, scope, and standard (NSW Food Authority, third-party GFSI, etc.).
  • Review previous audit report and verify all corrective actions are closed.
  • Update Food Safety Program if processes have changed.
  • Calibrate all thermometers and check temperature logs are complete.
  • Confirm pest control service is current and reports are filed.
  • Ensure all staff have current food safety supervisor certificates (NSW requirement).
  • Print and display the NSW Food Authority "Food Safety â€“ It's Your Responsibility" poster.

Documentation Checklist

  • Food Safety Program (HACCP plan) â€” signed, dated, version-controlled.
  • Receiving temperature logs (last 3 months minimum).
  • Cooking, cooling, and reheating temperature logs.
  • Transport temperature logs (if applicable).
  • Cleaning schedule and daily cleaning records.
  • Training matrix and individual training records.
  • Supplier approval register.
  • Corrective action and incident reports.
  • Pest control service reports and bait station map.
  • Waste disposal records (especially for grease traps).
  • Glass and hard plastic register (if applicable).
  • Allergen management documentation and matrix.

Physical Premises Checklist

  • Hand wash basins unobstructed, stocked, and within 5 m of all food handling areas.
  • All refrigeration units at correct temperature with visible, accurate thermometers.
  • No food stored directly on the floor.
  • Raw meats stored below and away from ready-to-eat foods.
  • All food contact surfaces in clean, sound condition (no cracked cutting boards, no rust).
  • Pest-proofing intact: no gaps around pipes, doors seal properly, screens on windows.
  • Wash-up area: three-compartment sink with sanitiser at correct concentration.
  • Waste bins foot-operated, lined, and covered.
  • Chemical storage separate from food, with current SDS available.
  • Lighting adequate and protected (shatter-proof covers in food zones).
  • Ventilation systems clean and functioning.
  • Toilet facilities clean, stocked, with hand washing signage.

Staff Knowledge Checklist

  • Food safety supervisor certificate displayed and current.
  • All food handlers know the three critical temperatures: fridge â‰¤ 5Â°C, freezer â‰¤ -15Â°C, cook â‰¥ 75Â°C.
  • Staff can demonstrate correct hand washing technique.
  • Staff understand allergen cross-contact risks and how to avoid them.
  • Staff know the corrective action procedure for temperature breaches.
  • Staff can locate and use the probe thermometer correctly.

Common NSW Audit Non-Conformances and How to Avoid Them

Analysis of NSW Food Authority enforcement data shows that the most common audit findings are:

  • Temperature control failures (34% of non-conformances): Incorrect fridge temperatures, inadequate cooling logs, and lack of temperature monitoring during transport.
  • Cleaning and sanitation gaps (22%): Incomplete cleaning records, dirty equipment, and lack of sanitising procedures.
  • Pest control deficiencies (15%): No pest control contract, missing bait station maps, or evidence of pest activity.
  • Food handler training gaps (12%): Expired or missing food safety supervisor certificates, no evidence of induction training.
  • Documentation errors (10%): Outdated Food Safety Program, incomplete logs, incorrect version control.

How often will my NSW food business be audited?
This depends on your risk classification. Low-risk retail businesses (e.g., packaged goods only) may be audited every 2â€“4 years by the local council. High-risk manufacturers, hospitals, and businesses handling potentially hazardous foods may be audited annually or even more frequently by the NSW Food Authority. Third-party GFSI-certified businesses typically undergo annual surveillance audits plus a full recertification audit every 2â€“3 years.
What happens if I fail a food safety audit?
A failed audit (major non-conformance) triggers a follow-up inspection within a specified timeframe â€” often 14â€“30 days. For NSW Food Authority audits, ongoing non-compliance can lead to prohibition orders, fines, or prosecution. For third-party certification audits, failure means loss of certification, which can bar you from supplying major retailers. In all cases, our team can step in with emergency consulting support to help you resolve findings quickly.
Can I do my own internal audit before the official one?
Absolutely â€” and we strongly recommend it. An internal audit is your best tool for catching issues before a regulator does. Internal audits should be conducted at least quarterly for high-risk businesses and bi-annually for lower-risk operations.
