Food Safety Supervisor Requirements NSW 2026

New South Wales has the most stringent Food Safety Supervisor (FSS) requirements in Australia. Under the NSW Food Regulation 2015 and the Food Standards Code, most NSW food businesses must appoint at least one trained and certified Food Safety Supervisor. The requirement applies to businesses across all sectors including restaurants, cafes, takeaway shops, bakeries, caterers, childcare centres, aged care facilities, hospitals, and food manufacturers.

As of 2026, the NSW Food Authority continues to mandate that every FSS must hold a Statement of Attainment from a nationally recognised training unit of competency. This guide covers who needs an FSS, the required certification levels, approved training providers, renewal periods, and the consequences of non-compliance.

Who Needs an FSS in NSW?

Under cl. 11 of the NSW Food Regulation 2015, the following business types must appoint an FSS:

  • Restaurants, cafes, and takeaway food businesses
  • Hotels and pubs serving food
  • Bakeries and pastry shops
  • Catering operations
  • Childcare centres and preschools that prepare and serve food
  • Aged care facilities
  • Hospitals and healthcare food services
  • Manufacturers and processors of potentially hazardous food
  • Wholesale food suppliers
  • Supermarkets and grocery stores with food preparation areas

Some businesses are exempt, including those that sell only pre-packaged, shelf-stable foods. However, if your business handles, prepares, or serves any potentially hazardous food, you almost certainly need an FSS.

Certification Levels and Units of Competency

The NSW Food Authority requires FSS certification through a registered training organisation (RTO). The specific units depend on your business sector:

Sector A — Hospitality

  • SITXFSA005 — Use hygienic practices for food safety
  • SITXFSA006 — Participate in safe food handling practices

Sector B — Retail

  • SIRRFSA001 — Handle food safely in retail

Sector C — Health and Community Services

  • HLTFSE001 — Follow basic food safety practices
  • HLTFSE007 — Oversee day-to-day food safety implementation

The FSS must hold the highest-level supervisory unit for their sector.

FSS Statutory Responsibilities

  • Supervising all food handling during operating hours
  • Ensuring staff are trained in safe food handling
  • Monitoring and recording temperature controls
  • Implementing and maintaining the food safety program
  • Taking corrective actions when breaches are identified
  • Acting as primary contact for council or NSW Food Authority

Your FSS should be someone who works regular shifts. Many small businesses choose the owner-operator or senior chef. Each business location requires its own FSS. Our consulting team can help you determine the right FSS structure.

Approved Training Providers

FSS certification must be obtained through a registered training organisation (RTO) approved by the NSW Food Authority. Training typically takes 1–2 days and costs between $150 and $400 per person. AMES Food Advisory provides in-person food safety training for FSS candidates across NSW. We deliver training at your premises for groups of 5 or more.

FSS Renewal Period

FSS certification in NSW does not have a formal expiry date. However, the NSW Food Authority recommends refreshing your training every 3 to 5 years to stay current with regulatory changes. If your FSS leaves the business, you must appoint a replacement as soon as practicable. We recommend having at least two trained staff members to avoid gaps in coverage.

Consequences of Non-Compliance

Operating without a certified FSS breaches the NSW Food Regulation 2015. Penalties include:

  • Penalty infringement notices of up to $880 for individuals and $2,200 for corporations
  • Court-imposed penalties of up to $55,000 for individuals and $275,000 for corporations
  • Enforcement orders to cease operations until compliance is achieved

FSS Record-Keeping Requirements

  • Statement of Attainment for your FSS
  • FSS contact details and availability schedule
  • Evidence of refresher training
  • Contingency plan for FSS absence

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a Food Safety Supervisor if I run a small takeaway shop?

Yes. Under cl. 11 of the NSW Food Regulation 2015, any business that handles potentially hazardous food must have an FSS. This includes small takeaway shops, even if you only employ yourself and one other person. The FSS can be the business owner as long as they hold the required certification.

Can I complete FSS training online?

Some RTOs offer blended delivery with online theory followed by an in-person assessment. However, the practical assessment component must be conducted face-to-face by a qualified trainer assessor. AMES Food Advisory offers on-site training and assessment at your own premises, ensuring the training is contextualised to your actual kitchen environment.

What happens if my FSS is on leave?

You must have a contingency plan. The NSW Food Authority expects that another trained and certified staff member can step in during the absence. If no certified backup is available, you may need to temporarily reduce your menu or cease certain food handling activities until the primary FSS returns.
