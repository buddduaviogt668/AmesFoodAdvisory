What you need in place before opening a new cafe in NSW

Opening a cafe in New South Wales involves several food safety compliance steps that must be completed before you begin trading. Missing any of these can result in operating illegally or receiving a non-compliance notice at your first audit.

  1. NSW Food Authority notification: Retail food businesses must notify the NSW Food Authority before commencing food handling activities. This is done through the NSW Food Authority's online portal and typically involves paying a notification fee and providing details of your business and premises.
  2. Food Safety Program: As a Class 2 food business, you need a documented HACCP-based Food Safety Program in place before opening. The FSP must be available for inspection at any time.
  3. Food Safety Supervisor: You must have a certified FSS in place from day one and notify the NSW Food Authority of their details.
  4. Staff training: All food handlers must have appropriate food safety skills and knowledge before working unsupervised with food.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

When do I need to notify the NSW Food Authority — before or after opening?
Before opening. Under the NSW Food Act 2003, food businesses must notify the NSW Food Authority before commencing food handling activities. Operating without notification is an offence. The notification is completed online through the NSW Food Authority website and typically requires details of the business name, address, type of food handled, and estimated start date.
Can I trade while my HACCP plan is being developed?
Technically, you must have a Food Safety Program in place before operating as a Class 2 food business in NSW. In practice, many new businesses begin with a draft FSP and refine it during initial operation. AMES recommends having a complete, site-specific FSP ready before your first service — the risk of trading without one, even briefly, is not worth the compliance exposure.
